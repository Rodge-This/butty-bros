## 1. THE STRATEGIC GOAL (read first)

**This website exists to grow the outdoor CATERING arm.** The shop is the origin story and the proof of quality; catering is what we're selling.

**Honest current scale (drives everything):** Butty Bros is a well-liked, growing local business, not yet a large-scale operation. The catering they realistically win *now* is:

- Birthdays and celebrations
- Funerals and wakes
- Christenings, garden parties, family gatherings
- Small office / workplace catering (breakfast drops, staff lunches)
- Community events, club days, fundraisers

**Lead with those.** Bigger stuff (festivals, film/TV crews, large corporate) stays on the site as "we also cater for…" — genuinely offered, not the headline. Leading with festival/film scale they can't yet evidence reads as over-claiming and speaks past the person actually about to enquire (someone organising a wake or a 30th). Reordering to match reality makes the site MORE likely to convert, not less.

**Register for the catering audience:** calm competence over loud energy. Someone booking a funeral wake or an office breakfast wants reassurance, reliability, warmth — not lightning bolts. Keep the personality in the *words*; keep the *visuals* clean.

## 3. THE REAL VOICE (pulled from their own Facebook posts)

Butty Bros talk in a **warm, cheeky, confident, generous Northern** voice. Emoji-forward on social, but the underlying tone translates to the website as: down-to-earth, self-assured, unpretentious, genuinely friendly.

**Actual phrases from their page (the tone bible):**
- "🚨 Hungry? Sorted. 🚨"
- "Big portions. Big flavour. Don't just scroll… come eat 👀"
- "IT'S SCRAN TIME!!"
- "HUNGOVER? CBA going to work after that amazing win? WE'VE GOT YOU!"
- "When other establishments start to copy your menu… all jokes aside we just want everyone to do well — there's enough bread" ← generous, funny, self-assured. This IS the brand.
- "OUTSIDE CATERING! PARTIES! EVENTS! MEETINGS! … WE CATER FOR ALL OCCASIONS!" ← their own catering framing (from their catering poster)
- "CALL US TODAY! LET'S TALK BUTTYS!" ← their actual catering call-to-action, in their voice. Candidate for the catering-page / enquiry CTA.

**Voice rules for the site:**
- Confident but warm. Never corporate, never salesy, never "premium" in a stuffy way.
- Northern plain-speaking: "proper," "sorted," "scran," "grub" — used lightly, not laid on thick.
- Generous and community-minded (the "enough bread" post shows real character — reflect that).
- For catering pages specifically: dial the slang DOWN a notch, dial reassurance UP. The energy stays in word choice; the message is "we'll turn up, cook great food, and make your event easy."
- Keep existing site copy where it already matches this — much of it does.

## 4. VISUAL / STYLE RULES (the logo settles the direction)

**The real logo** (now reconstructed as `images/logo-mark.svg`): solid black disc, double thin inner ring, "BUTTY BROS" in a heavy geometric sans (Montserrat/Poppins family), white on black. **Monochrome, clean, restrained.**

**Key insight:** their brand has THREE registers —
1. **The logo** — clean, black, restrained
2. **Shop illustrations** (cover art) — playful hand-drawn comic food, warm
3. **Deals graphics** — loud Impact caps, lightning bolts, yellow/red/black, high energy

The website should use **the LOGO's restraint as the master key**, borrow **warmth** from the shop illustrations, and save the **loud energy** for small accents only. This is not inventing a new brand — it's selecting the most refined existing thread, which is exactly right for catering buyers. Presenting a polished version of them to the catering market, while the loud energy lives on their socials, is the correct split.

**This vindicates the "tone it down / more premium" brief** — a premium, restrained site is TRUER to their logo than their loud promo tiles are. The tone-down is an elevation, not a departure.

### Concrete design rules
- Existing palette tokens are good and on-brand — keep them. `--iron` (near-black), `--paper` (greaseproof), `--sauce` (red), `--yolk` (gold), `--sage` (green).
- **Red discipline:** currently the red does everything (eyebrows, buttons, prices, marquee, rules, step numbers, focus, CTA band). Pull it back to earn its place — primary CTA + one or two accents. Everything else steps back to ink/charcoal/neutral. Restraint = premium.
- **Hero:** tone down. Headline drops from max size; lose the red-on-a-word; more whitespace; let any real photo read as a photo, not murk. (Owner wants 1–2 options before committing — still to do.)
- **Reduce the scroll.** Owner explicitly wants a tighter site — effective and nice, not an endless doom-scroll. Trim/merge sections; every section must earn its place.
- **All-caps mono labels** everywhere currently — let some breathe (small-caps / lighter weight) so it reads calmer.
- Logo now works on dark header (black disc melts into `--iron`, rings + text read) and on light.
